### Ceremony elimination and trainer liveness

The training lane had accumulated 24 instances of prologue ceremony: repeated directional verification, monitor loops, and resume checks before useful optimizer work. The audited purge reduced the mandatory prologue to one provenance gate, removed duplicate direction reruns, and made progress evidence mechanical.

The durable laws are:

1. verify immutable lineage once per launch, not continuously;
2. enter the optimizer promptly after the gate;
3. require first optimizer-entry evidence and periodic checkpoint/progress receipts;
4. resume only from a complete atomic checkpoint + sidecar;
5. do not return before critical rebound identity/checkpoint assignments;
6. retain exact failure stderr and stop at an atomic boundary;
7. a monitor cannot substitute for a live trainer.

One accelerated full-update implementation may be physically attempted under the BANANA spec. No complete accelerated-update timing is claimed in this publication.

## 3. July-29 serving campaign: forensic anatomy

### The starting false path

The first serving loop appeared to exercise a native prepacked route but actually pointed `VLLM_MOE_W2_PREPACKED_DIR` at a stub containing metadata and zero plane arrays. The installed runtime had also been reverted to a version that did not read that variable. Every boot silently used slow Python grouping/dispatch.

This made several configuration experiments misleading:

- decode graph flags were disabled in some launchers;
- `--enforce-eager` was hardcoded in another, disabling graph capture;
- a launch retained a capture-unsafe M-block route;
- malformed compilation JSON was introduced by concurrent launcher edits;
- MTP changed the capture shape from T=1 to T=2 and entered an unsupported path.

The campaign stopped treating flags as the whole problem and proved the actual route, payload, and runtime source at every layer.

### Format and state seam

The source TRUE-C export and the fastest historical serving layout did not share a lossless on-disk representation. A lossy converter was rejected. The accepted mechanism was lossless in-memory consolidation at load time:

- fused13 `915.440 → 318.672 µs` (`2.8727×`);
- down `455.808 → 179.456 µs` (`2.5399×`);
- aggregate `1371.248 → 498.128 µs` (`2.7528×`);
- minimum cosine `0.999998629`;
- source bytes unchanged and no transformed disk artifact.

Physical receipt SHA-256: `5e2d62bc0385424b140340e56ed9462a87ee368634d46683c0235067056f2485`.

Later concurrency work exposed the inverse compatibility problem: copying an older concurrency-fixed module wholesale removed the P943 `p943_native_all_family` state registration and failed at `KeyError: planes13`. The correct rule is to forward-port the small reviewed guard into the P943 adapter, not to replace the product adapter with a pre-P943 file.

### Warmup and five-row protocol

The cold-to-warm curve required three unmeasured decode requests before steady state. The frozen READY sequence is:

1. three unmeasured decode256 warmups;
2. one exact-2048 prefill warmup;
3. one exact-8192 prefill warmup;
4. health/model/provenance/memory/swap recheck;
5. five consecutive usage-counted decode256 rows;
6. exact-2048 and exact-8192 measured rows;
7. semantic/logit repeat probe;
8. one combined same-boot receipt.

A warm cache may be baked only after binding its cache manifest to the exact runtime/architecture. READY still requires the warm gate.
